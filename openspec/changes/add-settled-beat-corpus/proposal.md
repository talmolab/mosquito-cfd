# Proposal: add-settled-beat-corpus

Advances #192. Part of the post-lab-meeting queue (#197). Folds in #101 and #103.

## Why

Both force-surrogate corpora (`examples/prelim_sweep`, 64×32×64, and `examples/prelim_sweep_fine`,
256×128×256; 27 configurations each) are 2-wingbeat runs, and every consumer uses `wingbeat >= 1`,
which today means the second beat. That beat is not settled:

- Coarse grid, 6-beat local runs (exploration branch `explore-surrogate-experiments`, E0b). The
  numbers are the RMS difference from beat 6 over beat 6's peak, per target:
  - `s45_f100_p45`: beat 2 is 0.8–2.9% away, beat 4 is under 0.5%.
  - `s45_f100_p30`: beat 2 is 0.8–5.0% away, beat 4 is 0.3–1.6%, beat 5 is 0.1–0.6%. Beat 6 itself
    still moves 0.6% per beat in `CF_z`, so beat 6 is not a clean reference.
- Fine grid: beat 1 and beat 2 differ by 5–17% of peak (median over 27 configurations). No fine
  run is longer than 2 beats, so **the fine-grid settling rate is unmeasured**.
- The force surrogate's held-out error is 1.1–1.8% of peak. The unsettled residue is the same
  size, so it caps the accuracy any surrogate can be judged to.

Every later number (bench PR 2, the coarse-vs-fine figures, #194, #195) depends on a corpus whose
training beat is settled, and the beat count cannot be chosen without fine-grid data.

## What changes

Two stages in one change, delivered as three pull requests. Nothing runs without the user's
explicit go-ahead at each launch.

### Stage 1: fine-grid settling check (PR 1, then runs on gh-04)

1. **A settling metric** (`src/mosquito_cfd/force_surrogate/settling.py`, driver
   `scripts/check_settling.py`): per beat and per target, the step change from the previous beat,
   the distance to the last beat, a bound on how far the last beat still is from the periodic
   state, and the verdict. Definitions are in `design.md` D2.
2. **A pre-registered criterion**, committed and merged **before any run starts**
   (`examples/settling_check/PREREGISTRATION.md`, with the constants pinned in code, spec and a
   test): a beat is settled when its estimated distance to the periodic state is at most **1.0% of
   peak for all six targets**; the corpus beat count is the largest such beat over all runs
   (worst case, not the mean). The table of outcomes and what each one picks is in `design.md` D3.
3. **Long-run decks** for three configurations on both grids, generated from the committed corpus
   decks with a minimal, tested diff (`examples/settling_check/`):

   | configuration | why | beats |
   |---|---|---|
   | `s45_f100_p30` | slowest-settling configuration known | 8 |
   | `s45_f100_p45` | centre of the grid | 6 |
   | `s55_f115_p30` | high-frequency corner; also the CFL-worst configuration | 6 |

4. **Controls before the long runs**: a 400-step control against the committed cluster run, a
   restart-equivalence check, and afterwards a check that each long run's first two beats
   reproduce the committed corpus (`design.md` D4).
5. **Field settling, measured and reported but not part of the pass/fail criterion**: the fine
   runs write 20 plotfiles per beat; the near-wing velocity change between matched phases of
   successive beats is reported for the two `f* = 1.00` configurations.

### Stage 2: regenerate both corpora (PR 2 tooling and decision, cluster runs, PR 3 data)

6. **New corpus directories**, `examples/settled_sweep` (coarse) and
   `examples/settled_sweep_fine`, generated by the existing drivers with a new `--n-wingbeats`
   flag. The 2-beat corpora stay frozen and every test on them keeps passing, so each downstream
   consumer can move to the new corpus in its own later change (`design.md` D5).
7. **The training beat is the final requested beat** (`wingbeat == n_wingbeats - 1`), defined once
   in `settling.py` and in the spec. The extractor keeps every beat in the parquet, as it does
   today.
8. **Field capture designed for later visualization** (`design.md` D7): 20 plotfiles per beat at
   matched phase for every configuration and every beat, on **both** grids, and one checkpoint per
   beat boundary so any beat can be re-run later at a denser cadence.
9. **Unchanged solver settings**: `ns.fixed_dt = 5e-4`; fine `ns.cfl = 0.6`; coarse keeps its
   base-deck `ns.cfl = 0.3`, which has about 4× headroom at 64³. Run-observed metadata, the
   acceptance gate and the corpus guards apply to both new corpora; the coarse corpus gets
   per-config run metadata for the first time.
10. **Cluster sizing from step counts (#101)**: the deadline, the retry budget and the smoke step
    count are derived from the manifest's `max_step` values and measured seconds per step, in
    place of constants calibrated on a 2-beat run.
11. **A tested writer for `supersession_history` (#103)**.
12. **A settling check on all 54 regenerated runs** in the acceptance gate, with its result
    recorded in `sweep_provenance.json`.
13. **A storage inventory**, `storage_manifest.json`, committed in each new corpus directory:
    where the raw runs live, how large they are, and hashes of the small files (`design.md` D8).

### Downstream reruns (list only; each is its own later change)

`design.md` D10 lists them with the code each one needs: both force surrogates, both evidence
figures, the three coarse-vs-fine figures, V3, bench PR 2, README tables, the field-surrogate
reader's corpus pointer, and retiring the 2-beat corpora.

## Cost

| item | where | estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 fine runs (38,435 steps) | gh-04, RTX A5000 | about 80 h at 7.5 s/step; measured in the control before the long runs start |
| Stage 1 coarse twins (38,435 steps) | gh-04 | 2–20 h; the two rates on record differ 12×, the control settles it |

| Stage 2 fine corpus, N beats | cluster A40, parallelism 3 | 70.7 GPU-h × N/2: **177 (N=5), 212 (N=6), 283 (N=8) GPU-h**; about 2.2 to 3.6 days of wall time |
| Stage 2 coarse corpus | cluster A40 | well under 20 GPU-h (measured in its smoke run) |
| Stage 2 disk at N=6 | `Z:` cluster share (about 36 TB free) | about 2.0 TB fine + about 0.03 TB coarse |

## Impact

- **Specs**: `force-surrogate` (new settling, storage and supersession requirements; modified
  run-duration, deadline auto-scale and retry-budget requirements).
- **Code**: new `force_surrogate/settling.py`; small additions to `sweep.py`, `corpus_guards.py`,
  `acceptance_gate.py`, the two corpus drivers, `cluster/argo/`, and the
  `/submit-cluster-sweep` runbook. **Not touched**: `src/mosquito_cfd/surrogate_bench/`, the
  `add-surrogate-comparison-bench` change, `train.py`, the figure modules, PR #198.
- **Data**: three new example directories. The 2-beat corpora and everything derived from them
  are unchanged by this change.
- **Hardware**: this workstation's GPU is not used. Stage 1 runs on gh-04; Stage 2 on the cluster.

## Open questions for approval

1. **New directories or in-place regeneration** (D5). Recommended: new directories. In-place
   regeneration would break about a dozen committed-data tests at once and force every downstream
   rerun into the same pull request.
2. **Tolerance** (D3). 1.0% of peak is the issue's example. 0.5% would sit clearly below the
   surrogate error but, from the coarse decay rate, costs roughly 2 more beats per run.
3. **Coarse field capture** (D7). Recommended: on (about 26 GB). The coarse corpus has been
   force-only until now.
4. **Long-term home for raw runs** (D8). Recommended: the cluster share `Z:` as the working copy.
   Whether `Z:` is backed up, and whether a second copy on `G:` (27 TB free) is wanted, is the
   user's call.
5. **Resume-on-retry** (D9). A preempted 6-beat fine pod loses up to 13 h. This change measures
   whether a restart is equivalent but does not add resume; that would be a follow-up issue.
